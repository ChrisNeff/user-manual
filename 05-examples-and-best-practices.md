# Examples and Best Practices for Personal User Manuals

Learn from others and follow these best practices to create an effective Personal User Manual.

## Best Practices

### Be Authentic and Vulnerable

#### Share Real Quirks and Limitations
Instead of: "I'm a perfectionist" (humble brag)
Try: "I sometimes get too deep in the details and can slow down progress. Please tell me when I'm doing this."

#### Admit What You're Learning
- "I'm working on being less reactive in discussions"
- "I'm learning to delegate more effectively"
- "I struggle with giving critical feedback and am actively working on this"

### Be Specific and Actionable

#### Vague
"I value open communication."

#### Specific
"I prefer to hear bad news early. If something isn't going well, please tell me directly via Slack DM or in person. I'd rather know about problems when they're small than wait until they're big."

### Use Plain Language

#### Avoid Jargon
Write like you're talking to a friend, not writing a corporate memo.

#### Keep Sentences Short
Make it easy to scan and understand.

#### Use Examples
When explaining your preferences, give concrete examples.

## Common Sections from Real User Manuals

### Communication Examples

#### Messaging Preferences
"I'm in Slack from 9am-6pm PT. I check sporadically on evenings and weekends, but don't expect responses. For urgent matters outside these hours, text me at [number]."

#### Meeting Preferences
"I keep my calendar public. If you see an open slot and want to meet, just grab it—no need to ask. I block focus time in 2-hour chunks; please avoid scheduling over these unless urgent."

#### Email Approach
"I'm terrible at email. If it's important, please Slack me or put it on my calendar. If you email me and don't get a response in 48 hours, please follow up—I probably missed it."

### Feedback Examples

#### How to Give Feedback
"I want critical feedback as much as praise. Best time is during our check-ins, but if it's urgent, don't wait. I might get defensive initially—that's my problem, not yours. Please push through it."

#### How They Give Feedback
"I tend to give feedback in the moment. If I seem critical, it's because I care about quality, not because I'm upset. I'm working on balancing this with more positive feedback."

### Working Style Examples

#### Decision Making
"I prefer to make decisions quickly. This means I sometimes decide before having all the information. If you think I'm missing something important, please speak up—I'd rather slow down than make a bad decision."

#### Autonomy Level
"I value autonomy and trust people to make their own decisions. I want to know about major changes that affect me before they happen, but you don't need my approval on most things."

### Values Examples

#### Core Values
"Three things I care about most: 1) Delivering value to users, 2) Supporting each other's growth, 3) Being transparent about challenges. If you feel we're not living these, please call it out."

#### Collaboration Culture
"I believe in working sustainably. If you're consistently working nights or weekends on joint projects, something is wrong—either with our planning, priorities, or scope. Let's address it together."

## Sample Structures

### Minimal User Manual (Good for Starting Out)

```markdown
# Working with [Your Name]

## What I Do
[Your role and expertise]

## Communication
- How to reach me: [channels and preferences]
- Response times: [expectations]
- Check-ins: [frequency and format]

## Feedback
- How I give feedback: [your approach]
- How to give me feedback: [what works for you]

## My Quirks
[2-3 things people should know about working with you]

## What I'm Working On
[1-2 areas where you're trying to improve]
```

### Comprehensive User Manual

Include all sections from the [What to Include](03-what-to-include.md) document, but keep each section concise.

## Examples of Good Opening Statements

### Setting the Tone
"This document is my attempt to give you a head start on working together. It's not a replacement for getting to know each other—it's a starting point. Please ask questions and call me out when my actions don't match what I've written here."

### Showing Vulnerability
"I'm still learning and growing. This user manual is as much for me as it is for you—writing down what I believe helps me live up to it. I update this regularly as I learn, and I welcome your feedback on what I should add or change."

### Inviting Collaboration
"This is version 1.0 of working with me. You'll quickly find things I've missed or gotten wrong. When you do, please tell me so I can update this. Even better, create your own user manual and share it with our collaborators."

## Red Flags to Avoid

### Too Prescriptive
"You must send me daily updates by 5pm" → This sounds demanding and inflexible

### Too Vague
"I value collaboration" → Everyone says this; it's meaningless without specifics

### All Strengths, No Weaknesses
If you only share good things about yourself, it comes across as inauthentic.

### Making Excuses
"I'm bad at responding to emails" is fine. "I'm bad at responding to emails, so you should just accept that" is not.

### Too Long
If it's more than 2-3 pages, it's too long. Keep it concise and scannable.

## Evolution Over Time

### Your First User Manual
- Will be incomplete
- Will have things you later change
- Will miss important topics
- That's okay! Ship it anyway.

### After 6 Months
- Add lessons learned from experiences
- Remove things that aren't working
- Get more specific based on real interactions
- Add FAQ based on questions you've received

### After a Year
- Reflect on how you've grown
- Update your working philosophy
- Add new sections based on feedback
- Remove outdated information

## The Test

A good Personal User Manual answers these questions:
- "How do I get help when I'm stuck on something we're working on together?"
- "How do I know if you're happy with our collaboration?"
- "When should I reach out immediately vs. wait for our next check-in?"
- "What do you care most about?"
- "What are you working on improving?"

If your user manual doesn't answer these, consider adding more specifics.

## Resources for Inspiration

Many professionals have shared their user manuals publicly. Search for:
- "Personal User Manual" on GitHub
- "Working with me" documents from various professionals
- "README" files in personal repositories
- Team documentation from open source projects

The best user manual is one that's authentic to you and useful to your collaborators. Don't copy others—use them as inspiration to create something that's genuinely yours.
