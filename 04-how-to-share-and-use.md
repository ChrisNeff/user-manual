# How to Share and Use Your Manager README

Creating the document is just the first step. Here's how to effectively share and use your Manager README.

## Sharing Your Manager README

### Initial Distribution

#### New Manager, Existing Team
- Share it in a team meeting
- Walk through it together
- Invite questions and feedback
- Make it clear that it's a work in progress

#### Existing Manager, New Team Member
- Share it before their first day or during onboarding
- Reference it in your first 1:1
- Use it as a conversation starter
- Encourage them to ask questions

#### New Manager, New Team
- Share it as part of the team formation process
- Set the tone for open communication
- Use it to establish expectations early

### Where to Keep It

#### Make It Accessible
- Team wiki or knowledge base
- Shared drive or documentation system
- Team repository (if you work in tech)
- Wherever your team naturally looks for documentation

#### Keep It Visible
- Link to it in your email signature
- Reference it in team documentation
- Mention it during onboarding
- Update your team when it changes

## Using Your Manager README

### As a Conversation Starter

#### First 1:1s
- Use it to kickstart discussions about working styles
- Ask team members to share their own preferences
- Identify areas where you might need to adapt

#### Team Building
- Encourage team members to create their own READMEs
- Share and discuss as a team
- Find common ground and areas where adaptation is needed

### As a Reference Tool

#### During Conflicts
- Refer back to stated preferences when miscommunications happen
- Use it to understand whether expectations were clear
- Adjust the document if it wasn't clear enough

#### During Reviews
- Evaluate yourself against your stated values and practices
- Ask for feedback on whether you're living up to your README
- Update based on growth and changes

### Encouraging Team READMEs

#### Lead by Example
By sharing yours first, you make it safe for others to share theirs.

#### Provide a Template
Your README can serve as a template for team members who want to create their own.

#### Make It a Team Exercise
- Dedicate time for everyone to create their READMEs
- Share and discuss as a team
- Use them to improve collaboration

## Maintaining Your README

### Regular Reviews

#### Quarterly Check-ins
- Review your README every quarter
- Update anything that's changed
- Add new insights or learnings
- Remove things that no longer apply

#### After Major Changes
- New role or responsibilities
- Team changes or reorganizations
- Personal growth or realizations
- Feedback from your team

### Incorporating Feedback

#### Ask for Feedback Directly
- "Is this still accurate?"
- "What am I missing?"
- "Where do my actions not match my README?"

#### Observe Indirect Feedback
- Notice where confusion still happens
- Pay attention to repeated questions
- Watch for patterns in miscommunications

#### Make Updates Public
- Let your team know when you update it
- Explain what changed and why
- Show that you're continuously improving

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Don't Make It a Rulebook
Your README should invite collaboration, not create a strict set of rules your team must follow.

### Don't Set and Forget
A README that never changes suggests you're not growing or learning.

### Don't Avoid Difficult Topics
Glossing over your weaknesses or avoiding tough subjects makes the README less useful.

### Don't Contradict Your Actions
If your behavior doesn't match what you've written, either change the behavior or change the README.

### Don't Make It Too Long
If it's too long, people won't read it. Keep it concise and scannable.

## The Ultimate Goal

Your Manager README should make it easier for people to work with you, not create barriers. Use it as a tool for:
- Opening conversations
- Building trust
- Improving communication
- Fostering collaboration

If it's not serving these purposes, it's time to revise it.
